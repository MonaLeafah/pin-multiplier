# One article. Seven ways in.

Start with an article you have permission to reuse. Write down its title, destination, audience, main idea, and three useful points. Keep the article’s actual claims and limitations intact.

Give the seven pins different jobs:

1. The big idea introduces the article’s central thought.
2. Start here offers a concrete beginning.
3. The checklist collects steps worth keeping.
4. A useful detail brings forward a specific observation.
5. One small action gives the reader something practical to try.
6. The perspective opens a different way to understand the same idea.
7. Save for later helps the reader recognize when the article will be useful again.

Use the workbench to shape the words, review the graphic, and write alt text that describes what is visible. If the pin includes a paid or affiliate relationship, add and review a disclosure that describes the actual relationship. This guide does not establish legal or platform compliance.

Review each destination before publishing. The fictional demo links to `example.com`; replace that with the real article. Plan dates are suggestions you can edit, and the workbench does not schedule or publish anything.

Download each graphic as PNG for use in your publishing workflow. SVG keeps the text graphic editable. Export CSV to organize the set in a spreadsheet. Save JSON to reopen the full plan later.

To measure a real trial, record the article, pin angle, publication date, impressions, saves, outbound clicks, and any purchases attributable to the destination. Write down the attribution method and comparison period. Report observed results separately from predictions.
