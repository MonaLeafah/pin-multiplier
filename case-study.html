SYSTEMS & AUTOMATIONS / CASE STUDY 01

From an article brief
to seven pin drafts.

The Pin Multiplier uses the title, main idea, and useful points you provide to make seven editable pin drafts. You review the copy and download the graphics with a publishing plan.

Try the fictional example ↗

The problem

A finished article often gets one promotional graphic. Making the next six can turn into another blank-page exercise: new copy, new files, new dates, and another place to lose track of the work.

The approach

I built a small local workbench around an article brief. Seven editorial angles provide structure. The creator keeps control of the language, checks each pin, and takes the files into their own publishing workflow.

01 / Article brief→02 / Seven angles→03 / Human review→04 / Files + plan

The demonstration

The fictional article is about making a creative corner in a small space. Its main idea and three useful points become seven editable pin drafts. Each draft includes a headline, supporting copy, alt text, a destination, and a planned date.

The tool makes text graphics at 1000 × 1500 pixels, downloads the complete seven-pin packet in one ZIP, and exports the plan as CSV or JSON. The packet also includes a blank results tracker. Saved JSON can be reopened. An edit returns a reviewed pin to draft status.

What the demo shows

The example demonstrates one brief becoming seven distinct editorial angles, with editable files and a review trail. It does not establish traffic, revenue, conversion improvement, or time saved. Those outcomes need a real publishing trial and measurement.

Why it stays local

The application uses deterministic templates and the text supplied in the brief. No account, API key, remote image processing, analytics, or automatic publishing is required. The sample content is original and fictional.
